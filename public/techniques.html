Cold Call

Invite a student to answer without relying on raised hands. Make the routine predictable, give thinking time and respond with warmth.

Before you start Explain the routine first: everyone prepares an answer, anyone may be invited, and help is available.

  1. 01

    1. Ask everyone

    Pose a clear question to the whole class before naming a student.

    Try saying“Why does increasing the temperature make evaporation faster?”
  2. 02

    2. Pause for thinking

    Give silent preparation time. Five seconds is a starting point; allow longer for a demanding question.

    Try saying“Everyone think first. Be ready to explain your reason.”
  3. 03

    3. Invite a response

    Name a student calmly. Listen to the answer without rushing to fill the silence.

    Try saying“Aroha, what do you think?”
  4. 04

    4. Build on the answer

    Check accuracy, probe the reasoning and invite others to add or improve.

    Try saying“What evidence supports that? Sam, what can you add?”

Keep it supportive

Use it routinely, rather than as a surprise consequence. Start with accessible questions and adapt preparation or response options to students’ needs.

Prepare. Rehearse. Discuss.

Think–Pair–Share

Give students an individual starting point, then a chance to rehearse with a partner before contributing to the class.

Before you start Set one focused question. Explain who starts, how partners take turns and what they will share afterwards.

  1. 01

    1. Think independently

    Everyone forms an answer first. A quick note or sketch can help. Choose enough time for the question.

    Try saying“Think quietly: which material would be the best insulator, and why?”
  2. 02

    2. Pair and rehearse

    Partners take turns explaining. The listener asks a question or helps improve the reason.

    Try saying“Partner A explains first. Partner B asks why. Then swap.”
  3. 03

    3. Share with the class

    Bring everyone back together. Invite a few contributions, compare ideas and resolve misconceptions.

    Try saying“Tell us your pair’s reasoning. Who reached a different conclusion?”

Keep the thinking in the routine

Allow individual thinking before talk. Circulate during pairing to hear the reasoning and choose useful ideas for the discussion.

See the whole class’s thinking.

Check Everyone

Collect an answer from every student, for example on mini whiteboards. Use the pattern of responses to decide what to teach next.

Before you start Choose a short question that will expose a specific misconception. Teach the write, cover and reveal routine first.

  1. 01

    1. Think and write

    Everyone answers independently. Ask for large, readable writing; keep answers private while others are thinking.

    Try saying“Which circuit has the brighter bulb? Write A or B and a reason.”
  2. 02

    2. Cover and get ready

    Students cover their answer, then hold the board ready on your signal. Avoid copying or early shouting out.

    Try saying“Cover your answer. Boards ready.”
  3. 03

    3. Reveal and scan

    Reveal together. Scan the whole room and look for common errors, not just a few correct boards.

    Try saying“Show me in 3, 2, 1. Hold still while I look.”
  4. 04

    4. Respond and recheck

    Ask a student to explain. If needed, model or reteach, then check everyone again with a fresh question.

    Try saying“Let’s compare these reasons, then try another example.”

A board check needs a response

If a misconception is common, pause and address it. If understanding is secure, move on or increase the challenge.

Use it in Cold Call Classroom

In Settings, choose Check Everyone and set the response time. Start the routine while students prepare answers. Scan all responses before asking the revealed student to explain.

Help, then return.

No Opt Out

When a student is unsure, provide support and return to them so they can complete a successful response.

Before you start Explain that uncertainty is useful information. The student will receive help and another chance, without embarrassment.

  1. 01

    1. Acknowledge the uncertainty

    Keep the tone calm. Find out whether they need more time, a reminder or help with the first step.

    Try saying“Which part can you start with?”
  2. 02

    2. Provide support

    Offer a precise hint, a model or a peer’s explanation. Give the original student time to listen or rehearse.

    Try saying“Use the diagram: where is the concentration higher?”
  3. 03

    3. Return to the student

    Invite them to answer again with the support. Check meaning; follow up beyond repeating words where appropriate.

    Try saying“Now explain the direction of diffusion and why it happens.”

Aim for a successful response

Keep the support proportionate. If the class lacks the knowledge needed, teach it before asking again.

AFTER THE FIRST ANSWER

Keep the thinking going.

Use a follow-up that fits what the answer tells you.

PROBE

“What makes you think that?”

ADD EVIDENCE

“What evidence supports your answer?”

BUILD

“What can you add to that answer?”

IMPROVE

“Can you make that explanation more precise?”
